MEN WHO CARRY SECRETS · WEEK 1

A Man’s Mouth Reveals His Character

Scripture: Matthew 12:36–37

“But I say unto you, That every idle word that men shall speak, they shall give account thereof in the day of judgment. For by thy words thou shalt be justified, and by thy words thou shalt be condemned.”

Most men understand accountability when their actions are visible. We know our decisions carry consequences, and we understand that people often judge our character by what we do. The Word of God reminds us, however, that our words carry accountability as well, including the conversations we have when nobody else is around.

The way a man talks about others reveals a great deal about his heart. There is a difference between the man who speaks about integrity publicly and the man who practices integrity privately. When the doors close, when another brother is absent, and when a conversation begins to drift into personal matters, our character is often revealed by what we choose to say.

Gossip rarely introduces itself as gossip. More often, the conversation begins with an explanation.

We can make a conversation sound innocent, even sincere, while crossing a line that should never have been crossed. Concern, prayer, humor, friendship, and even a desire to warn someone can become excuses for sharing information that was never ours to share. Good intentions do not automatically make a conversation right.

The real question is not whether the story was interesting, whether the information was true, or whether we felt justified in telling someone. We have to ask whether the person receiving the information actually needed to know, whether we had permission to share, and whether our responsibility as a brother called for speaking or remaining silent.

A man who wants to walk in integrity must learn to stop and examine his words before they leave his mouth. Some conversations are worth having. Others are better left alone.

Men’s Challenge

Before sharing something about another person, take a moment to examine yourself.

Does the person receiving this information genuinely need to know? Did the person involved give me permission to share? Am I trying to help, or am I simply looking for an opportunity to talk? Would I be comfortable having this same conversation with the person standing in front of me? Most importantly, could I stand before God and give an honest account of why I chose to say these words?

A man’s character is not only revealed by what he says when people are listening, but also by what he chooses to say when the person being discussed is absent.
