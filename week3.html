MEN WHO CARRY SECRETS · WEEK 3

3. Gossip Disguised as Concern

A prayer request should never become a spiritual permission slip for gossip.

4. A Man Who Cannot Control His Tongue Cannot Be Trusted With Greater Responsibility

Scripture: James 3:5–6

Many men desire leadership, influence, respect, and opportunities to serve. We want to be trusted, to make a difference, and to have a meaningful place in the work of God. Those desires are not wrong, but the responsibility that comes with leadership requires more than ability. A man must also possess the character to handle the responsibility placed in his hands.

Spiritual maturity cannot be separated from the discipline of our words. A man may be talented, knowledgeable, gifted, charismatic, and well respected, yet repeatedly exposing private conversations reveals a serious problem with integrity. Giftedness may open doors, but character determines whether a man can be trusted once he walks through them.

Sometimes the greatest test of maturity is not standing before a congregation and speaking confidently. Sometimes the greater test is sitting among friends, hearing something personal about another man, and having the discipline to keep that information to yourself.

A man who cannot protect what has been entrusted to him should carefully examine his readiness to carry responsibility for others. Leadership involves knowing what needs to be said, what needs to be addressed, and what should never leave a private conversation.

Discretion is part of leadership. Restraint is part of maturity. Wisdom includes recognizing that having information does not automatically give us permission to share it.

A man who wants to be trusted with greater responsibility must first demonstrate that he can be trusted with the conversations already entrusted to him.

Leadership requires discretion.

Leadership requires restraint.

Leadership requires the wisdom to know when speaking is necessary and when silence is the more faithful response.
