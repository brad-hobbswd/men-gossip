MEN WHO CARRY SECRETS · WEEK 6

6. The Spirit of Gossip Loves Information More Than Integrity

Scripture: Proverbs 18:8

“The words of a talebearer are as wounds, and they go down into the innermost parts of the belly.”

There are men who seem to have an appetite for information about everyone around them. They want to know who is struggling, who is arguing, who is leaving, who is being corrected, who made a mistake, who is having problems at home, what leadership said, what happened during a private meeting, and what somebody confessed when they believed their conversation was confidential.

The desire to know everything can become spiritually dangerous. Information may appear harmless, especially when presented as simple curiosity or concern, yet the heart must be examined when another person’s private circumstances become a source of fascination. Not every piece of information deserves our attention. Not every conversation deserves our participation. Not every secret deserves our curiosity.

A mature man understands the difference between knowledge that equips him for responsibility and information that merely satisfies his appetite. Wisdom seeks understanding for the purpose of doing what is right. Gossip seeks information that can be repeated, discussed, analyzed, or used to satisfy curiosity. One produces maturity. The other can feed pride, judgment, suspicion, and division.

There comes a point when curiosity is no longer innocent. A man may find himself asking questions that have no legitimate purpose, searching for details that do not concern him, or listening closely to information that another person’s dignity should have protected. The issue is no longer simply what he hears. The issue becomes what his heart desires to hear.

Proverbs 18:8 describes the words of a talebearer as wounds that reach deep within. Gossip does not always produce immediate visible damage. Sometimes the information settles into the heart of the listener, influencing how a person is viewed long after the conversation has ended. A reputation may be altered, a relationship may become strained, and an opinion may form before the person ever has an opportunity to speak for himself.

Men must learn to discipline their curiosity. Being informed about everything happening around us is not a sign of spiritual maturity. Knowing when information is none of our business is evidence of wisdom. A man does not need access to every private conversation, every personal struggle, or every detail of another person’s life to walk faithfully with God.

There is a difference between being available to help a brother and being eager to hear about his problems. There is a difference between carrying a burden in prayer and carrying information from one conversation to another. There is a difference between having concern for someone’s soul and satisfying a desire to know what happened.

Integrity sometimes requires a man to refuse information that he could easily obtain.

The mature man does not measure his value by how much he knows, how many people confide in him, or how many private matters reach his ears. He measures his character by how faithfully he handles what has been entrusted to him and how wisely he responds when information is offered that does not belong to him.

When curiosity begins pulling a man toward another person’s vulnerability, the appropriate response is not to pursue the information further. The appropriate response is to examine the heart.

A man who loves integrity more than information will know when to stop listening, when not to ask another question, and when to protect the dignity of someone who is not present to protect himself.
