7. The Man Who Repeats Confidential Information Must Examine His Own Integrity Scripture: Psalm 15:1–3 (KJV) “Lord, who shall abide in thy tabernacle? who shall dwell in thy holy hill? He that walketh uprightly, and worketh righteousness, and speaketh the truth in his heart. He that backbiteth not with his tongue, nor doeth evil to his neighbour, nor taketh up a reproach against his neighbour.” The question is not merely whether a man knows Scripture. The question is whether his conduct reflects the Scripture he knows. Spiritual maturity cannot be measured solely by how much a man can quote, teach, preach, or discuss. His character must also be examined through the way he treats people, handles private information, and conducts conversations when accountability is absent. A man can condemn gossip in a public teaching and participate in gossip privately. He can tell others to guard their tongues while repeating information that was entrusted to him. He can speak about holiness while entertaining conversations that wound another person’s reputation. He can preach unity from a pulpit while privately contributing to division among the very people he claims to love. Knowing the Word of God does not excuse disobedience to the Word of God. Spiritual knowledge carries responsibility. The more a man understands concerning righteousness, integrity, brotherhood, and holiness, the greater his responsibility becomes to live according to that understanding. Psalm 15 presents a sobering examination of the character of a man who desires to dwell in the presence of God. His walk matters. His righteousness matters. His speech matters. His treatment of his neighbor matters. The passage does not separate spiritual devotion from the integrity of a man’s everyday conduct. The Holy Ghost does not merely empower a man to speak. The Holy Ghost also teaches a man when silence is obedience. Spiritual maturity includes restraint, discernment, and the willingness to refuse conversations that compromise the character of Christ. There are conversations a man should refuse. There are details he should never request. There are stories he should never repeat. There are relationships he should protect rather than weaken. There are moments when a man must recognize that his participation would contribute nothing righteous to the conversation. Silence is not always weakness. Silence can demonstrate wisdom, discipline, loyalty, and spiritual maturity. A man who refuses to participate in gossip does not need to announce his superiority or condemn everyone involved. He simply recognizes the responsibility resting upon his own mouth and chooses not to contribute to the damage. The examination must become personal. A man cannot continually identify the failures of others while refusing to confront his own. The question is not merely whether gossip exists around him. The question is whether gossip has found a place within him. Does he repeat information that should have remained confidential? Does he entertain conversations that diminish another person? Does he protect the dignity of men who are absent? Does his private conduct agree with the spiritual principles he publicly professes? Integrity is revealed when a man’s private behavior agrees with his public confession. A man who desires the presence of God must take seriously the condition of his heart, the discipline of his tongue, and the responsibility of his relationships. The goal is not simply to avoid being caught speaking wrongly. The goal is to become a man whose heart has been so surrendered to God that he no longer desires to participate in conversations that dishonor another person. The most spiritual thing a man can say in some conversations is nothing at all.
