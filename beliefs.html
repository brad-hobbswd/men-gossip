The foundation is that our conduct, speech, brotherhood, holiness, and treatment of others must be governed by the doctrine taught by the apostles and the Word of God. This makes Men Who Carry Secrets a discipleship teaching rooted in Apostolic faith and Christian living. Biblical and Apostolic Foundation The Doctrine of the Apostles Must Govern Our Words and Conduct Acts 2:42 (KJV) “And they continued stedfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers.” The Apostolic Church was established upon doctrine, fellowship, prayer, and a commitment to live according to the teaching of Jesus Christ. Apostolic doctrine is not simply what we believe concerning God, salvation, baptism, and the Holy Ghost. The doctrine we receive must also shape the way we live, speak, treat one another, and conduct ourselves within the body of Christ. A man cannot separate his spiritual life from his daily conduct. The same Holy Ghost who empowers a man to worship, pray, witness, and serve also calls him to exercise discipline over his tongue, walk in humility, pursue holiness, love his brethren, and refuse conduct that damages the body of Christ. The sin of gossip directly conflicts with the biblical principles of holiness, brotherhood, truthfulness, charity, spiritual maturity, and unity. A man who claims Apostolic faith must examine not only what he believes, but whether his daily conduct reflects the doctrine he professes. Doctrinal Principles Governing This Teaching 1. Holiness in Our Conduct 1 Peter 1:15–16 God calls His people to holiness. Holiness reaches beyond outward appearance and religious activity. Our speech, attitudes, motives, private conversations, and treatment of others must also reflect a life separated unto God. 2. Discipline of the Tongue James 3:1–12 Scripture teaches that the tongue carries tremendous influence. A man must recognize the power of his words and accept responsibility for their effects. Spiritual maturity requires discipline over speech, refusing words that corrupt, injure, deceive, or destroy. 3. Love and Brotherhood John 13:34–35 Jesus identified love for one another as a defining mark of His disciples. Brotherhood cannot thrive where confidence is repeatedly violated, reputations are attacked, and private struggles become public conversations. Love requires protecting dignity, exercising compassion, and treating brothers with the same care we desire to receive. 4. Unity in the Body of Christ Ephesians 4:1–6 The Church is called to walk in unity, humility, patience, and love. Gossip, whispering, division, suspicion, and personal attacks work against the spiritual unity God desires among His people. 5. Restoration Rather Than Destruction Galatians 6:1–2 The responsibility of a spiritual brother is to restore someone overtaken in a fault with meekness, bearing burdens rather than increasing them. Correction must be motivated by restoration and governed by humility, not humiliation, exposure, or personal satisfaction. 6. Truthfulness and Integrity Ephesians 4:25, 29 Believers are called to speak truth and communicate in ways that edify. Truth must not become an excuse for careless disclosure, unnecessary exposure, or conversations that damage another person's reputation without serving a righteous purpose. 7. Separation From Corrupt Communication Ephesians 4:31–32 Bitterness, wrath, anger, evil speaking, and malice must be put away. The Apostolic life requires compassion, forgiveness, kindness, and a heart shaped by the Spirit of God. Apostolic Position and Purpose of This Teaching Men Who Carry Secrets is not a teaching that promotes hiding sin, protecting wrongdoing, avoiding biblical correction, or refusing accountability. Scripture calls believers to truth, repentance, correction, holiness, and restoration. The purpose is to distinguish righteous accountability from sinful gossip, legitimate correction from personal exposure, spiritual concern from curiosity, and biblical restoration from conversations that injure another person's dignity. A man must know when information requires appropriate action, when a brother needs correction, when a matter should be brought to spiritual leadership, and when private information must remain confidential. Wisdom requires discernment, not indiscriminate silence. The Apostolic standard is not simply, “Keep everything secret.” The standard is: Walk in holiness. Speak truth. Protect confidence. Correct in righteousness. Restore in meekness. Bear one another's burdens. Refuse division. Love your brother. Foundational Declaration We believe the doctrine of the apostles calls us to a life of holiness, truth, humility, spiritual discipline, brotherhood, and unity. Our speech must reflect the character of Jesus Christ and the work of the Holy Ghost within us. We refuse gossip, slander, whispering, malicious conversation, and the misuse of information entrusted to us. We commit ourselves to biblical accountability, righteous correction, restoration, and the protection of our brothers' dignity. We will not use another man's weakness as material for conversation. We will use our strength to help carry his burden.
