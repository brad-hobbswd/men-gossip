MEN'S DISCIPLESHIP · DOCTRINAL FOUNDATION

Biblical and Apostolic Foundation

The Doctrine of the Apostles Must Govern Our Words and Conduct

Acts 2:42
“And they continued stedfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers.”

The Apostolic Church was established upon doctrine, fellowship, prayer, and a commitment to live according to the teaching of Jesus Christ. Apostolic doctrine is not simply what we believe concerning God, salvation, baptism, and the Holy Ghost. The doctrine we receive must also shape the way we live, speak, treat one another, and conduct ourselves within the body of Christ.

A man cannot separate his spiritual life from his daily conduct. The same Holy Ghost who empowers a man to worship, pray, witness, and serve also calls him to exercise discipline over his tongue, walk in humility, pursue holiness, love his brethren, and refuse conduct that damages the body of Christ.

The sin of gossip directly conflicts with holiness, brotherhood, truthfulness, charity, spiritual maturity, and unity. A man who claims Apostolic faith must examine not only what he believes, but whether his daily conduct reflects the doctrine he professes.

Doctrinal Principles Governing This Teaching

1. Holiness in Our Conduct · 1 Peter 1:15–16

God calls His people to holiness. Our speech, attitudes, motives, private conversations, and treatment of others must reflect a life separated unto God.

2. Discipline of the Tongue · James 3:1–12

The tongue carries tremendous influence. Spiritual maturity requires discipline over speech, refusing words that corrupt, injure, deceive, or destroy.

3. Love and Brotherhood · John 13:34–35

Love for one another is a defining mark of Christ's disciples. Brotherhood requires protecting dignity, exercising compassion, and treating others with the care we desire to receive.

4. Unity in the Body of Christ · Ephesians 4:1–6

The Church is called to walk in unity, humility, patience, and love. Gossip, whispering, division, suspicion, and personal attacks work against that unity.

5. Restoration Rather Than Destruction · Galatians 6:1–2

Restore a brother overtaken in a fault with meekness, bearing burdens rather than increasing them. Correction must be motivated by restoration, not humiliation.

6. Truthfulness and Integrity · Ephesians 4:25, 29

Speak truth and communicate in ways that edify. Truth must not become an excuse for careless disclosure or conversations that damage another's reputation without righteous purpose.

7. Separation From Corrupt Communication · Ephesians 4:31–32

Put away bitterness, wrath, anger, evil speaking, and malice. The Apostolic life requires compassion, forgiveness, kindness, and a heart shaped by the Spirit of God.

Apostolic Position and Purpose

This teaching does not promote hiding sin, protecting wrongdoing, avoiding biblical correction, or refusing accountability. Scripture calls believers to truth, repentance, correction, holiness, and restoration.

We distinguish righteous accountability from sinful gossip, legitimate correction from personal exposure, spiritual concern from curiosity, and biblical restoration from conversations that injure another person's dignity. A man must discern when information requires appropriate action, when a brother needs correction, when a matter should be brought to spiritual leadership, and when private information must remain confidential. Wisdom requires discernment, not indiscriminate silence.

Walk in holiness. Speak truth. Protect confidence. Correct in righteousness. Restore in meekness. Bear one another's burdens. Refuse division. Love your brother.

Foundational Declaration

We believe the doctrine of the apostles calls us to a life of holiness, truth, humility, spiritual discipline, brotherhood, and unity. Our speech must reflect the character of Jesus Christ and the work of the Holy Ghost within us. We refuse gossip, slander, whispering, malicious conversation, and the misuse of information entrusted to us. We commit ourselves to biblical accountability, righteous correction, restoration, and the protection of our brothers' dignity. We will not use another man's weakness as material for conversation. We will use our strength to help carry his burden.
