MEN WHO CARRY SECRETS · WEEK 5

5. The Talebearer Does Not Merely Tell Stories. He Creates Division

Scripture: Proverbs 16:28

“A froward man soweth strife: and a whisperer separateth chief friends.”

Gossip rarely remains confined to the original conversation. Once information leaves the mouth of the person entrusted with keeping it, the story begins traveling beyond the control of the original speaker. Each person who hears the information may add an opinion, draw a conclusion, question someone’s motives, or repeat the story with a different emphasis. Before long, the original facts become buried beneath assumptions and interpretations.

Relationships begin to suffer. Men who once prayed together, worked together, worshipped together, and trusted one another may begin questioning the very relationships that once held them together. Suspicion replaces confidence, conversations become guarded, and the atmosphere of brotherhood begins to change.

The talebearer may eventually defend himself by saying, “I didn’t know they would take the conversation that way,” “I only told one person,” or “I never intended for this to happen.” Those explanations may sound reasonable to the person speaking, yet the consequences remain. A seed was planted, and the speaker cannot determine how deeply the seed will take root or how far the resulting damage will spread.

Proverbs 16:28 makes the connection unmistakable. A whisperer does not merely communicate information. A whisperer separates friends. The damage may begin with a seemingly innocent conversation, yet the consequences can reach relationships that took years to establish.

A man’s words can become the wedge that drives another man’s relationship apart. The separation may not happen immediately. The damage may become visible months later, when trust has weakened, communication has changed, and someone can no longer understand why the relationship feels different. The man who introduced the original conversation may never realize how far the consequences traveled.

Gossip is dangerous because the speaker may walk away from the conversation while others are left living with the damage.

Brotherhood requires men who are committed to preserving relationships rather than contributing to their destruction. A godly man should never be satisfied merely knowing that he spoke the truth. He must also examine whether his words served righteousness, protected another person’s dignity, and strengthened the relationships God entrusted to him.

The question is not simply whether the story was accurate. The question is whether the conversation was necessary, whether the information belonged to him to share, and whether his words helped preserve peace or planted seeds of division.

A man who values brotherhood must refuse to become the voice that separates brothers.
