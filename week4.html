MEN WHO CARRY SECRETS · WEEK 4

4. A Man Who Cannot Control His Tongue Cannot Be Trusted With Greater Responsibility

Scripture: James 3:5–6

“Even so the tongue is a little member, and boasteth great things. Behold, how great a matter a little fire kindleth! And the tongue is a fire, a world of iniquity: so is the tongue among our members, that it defileth the whole body, and setteth on fire the course of nature; and it is set on fire of hell.”

Many men desire leadership, influence, respect, and opportunities to serve. We want to be trusted with responsibility and have a meaningful place in the work of God. Those desires are understandable, but leadership requires more than talent, knowledge, or the ability to speak well. A man must also possess the character to handle the responsibility placed in his hands.

Spiritual maturity and verbal discipline go hand in hand. A man may be gifted, knowledgeable, charismatic, and influential, but those qualities cannot compensate for a lack of integrity. When a man repeatedly exposes private conversations, he demonstrates that something in his character needs attention.

Sometimes the greatest test of maturity is not standing behind a pulpit and speaking confidently. Sometimes the greater test comes when a brother shares something personal, a conversation begins to drift, and we have to decide whether to participate or exercise restraint. Knowing when to keep our mouths closed can reveal more about our maturity than knowing how to speak in front of a crowd.

A man who cannot protect information entrusted to him should seriously examine whether he is ready to carry greater responsibility over others. Leadership means understanding that not every piece of information belongs in every conversation. Some matters require wisdom, some require direct confrontation, some require prayer, and some require silence.

Discretion is part of leadership. Restraint is part of maturity. Wisdom is recognizing that having information does not automatically mean we have permission to share it.

A man who desires greater responsibility must first demonstrate that he can faithfully handle the responsibilities already entrusted to him.

Leadership requires discretion. It requires restraint. It requires the wisdom to know when a conversation is necessary and when silence is the better choice.

A man’s influence should never come at the expense of another man’s trust.
